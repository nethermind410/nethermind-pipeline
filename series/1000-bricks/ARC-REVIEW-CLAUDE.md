# 1,000 BRICKS — arc review and proposed fixes

- **From:** Claude Code, 23 Nov 2026, at Courtney's request ("Can we make it better??").
- **Status:** PROPOSAL. Nothing here is in force. `ARC.md`, `BRIEF.md` and `PRODUCTION-SPEC.md` are unchanged and remain the truth until Courtney approves a change and Muse writes it into the bible.
- **Scope:** the story and the format only. The hero asset, the consistency gate and the production spec are not touched.

## Verdict

The bones are good. The format around them will lose a Shorts audience.

**Keep:** the stone arm that opens the Door in the last episode; the Kind Voice being the previous him; the Lie-Bell; the statue with his face; bricks coming off his body; the two real endings.

**Fix:** the eight things below.

## What is wrong, and the fix for each

### 1. The votes don't decide anything
The arc has a "canonical path" and fixed numbers. Episode 6 "lands on 865 either way". Episode 8's two options arrive at the same corridor. Episode 11's two options cost the same. Zero is impossible until Episode 11.

Viewers will work that out by the third episode, and the whole pitch is "you decide".

**Fix:** every vote changes something the viewer can see in the first three seconds of the next episode: a piece on him, a piece off him, or who is standing next to him. The story beats can stay on rails. The count and the loadout must really move apart.

### 2. Nobody knows what the number needs to be
The count goes 1,000 → 640 over eleven episodes, then the Door takes nearly all of it by rule. So for eleven episodes the number never looks dangerous, and at the end it doesn't matter what it was.

**Fix:** carve the Door's price on the wall in Episode 1. **The Door costs 500.** Now every vote is maths the audience can do: "he's on 610, that costs 90, don't." The question all season is "will he have 500?", and the count finally means something.

### 3. The swings are too small
Most episodes move the count by 3 to 7 per cent. On a phone that reads as nothing happening.

**Fix:** swings of 60 to 200. Under 700 by the midpoint. Act 3 played within reach of 500, on either side of it.

### 4. The lore is hidden, and the lore is why people watch this channel
The arc says the source is never named on screen. But named lore is the thing that works here: the Jujutsu Kaisen, Bleach and One Piece Shorts mostly do 1,000 to 2,000 views, and the earlier original-world "Nether" Shorts did 4 to 505.

**Fix:** say it out loud, in the first ten seconds. "This room runs on a binding vow. If you've read Jujutsu Kaisen, you know how this ends." Put the series name in the title too: "Would You Let Mahoraga's Wheel Adapt You?"

### 5. It doesn't sound like Courtney
The narration is third person and solemn: "The corridor is one statue richer. It is always one statue richer." The hero is Courtney, and Courtney's voice is chaotic, kind and funny (see `VOICE.md` on PR #3).

**Fix:** first person, his voice. He is annoyed about losing bricks. He does the maths badly. He is polite to the imp. The dread lands harder when the person it is happening to is making jokes.

### 6. Each episode carries too much
Last vote, the split, a new room, a mechanic, a price, a hidden ledger move, one thread advanced, one planted, then the choice. In 140 to 190 words. And nine loadout pieces across twelve episodes.

**Fix:**
- 80 to 110 words, 30 to 40 seconds.
- One room, one price, one choice.
- Four loadout pieces, not nine: the stone arm, the bell, the red leg, the key. Drop the gauntlet, the price tag, the grey temples and the wheel as separate pieces.
- The stone arm creeps one plate further up his arm every episode. That is a second counter the viewer can read with the sound off.

### 7. A stranger can't join in the middle
Shorts are served out of order. Most people will meet this series at Episode 5 or 8.

**Fix:** the same cold open every episode, four seconds, in his voice: "I'm made of [count] bricks. The Door costs 500. Zero and I'm a statue. You lot decide."

### 8. It earns no watch hours
The series is Shorts-length, so it adds nothing to the 8,000 long-form hours the channel needs.

**Fix:** make it feed long-form.
- After each act, one long-form lore essay: "The Real Lore Behind 1,000 BRICKS, Act 1" (Hellboy's hand, binding vows, the symbiote, devil contracts). Every episode in that act points at it.
- After the finale, one long-form cut of the whole story with Courtney's commentary over it.

That is four long videos out of work already being done.

## Two practical problems

- **Vote counts will be tiny at 51 subscribers.** A "62% chose A" card built on five comments looks dead. Show the real number instead ("9 of you voted. 6 said take it."), and use a Community poll alongside the comments.
- **The next episode can't be built until the vote closes.** With the current gate that makes a daily episode impossible. Build both three-second consequence shots in advance and keep everything after them shared; run 48-hour votes, two episodes a week.

One thing for Courtney to check outside the story: the minifigure shape is a LEGO trademark, and this series is meant to earn money. Fan brickfilms are common, but it is worth knowing where LEGO's published fan-content rules draw the line before the series is monetised. I have not researched this.

## The twelve, revised

Same rooms, same order, same twist. What changes is in the last column.

| # | Episode | The vote | What changes from the arc |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Statue's Arm | Take the stone plate (+35, it starts growing) / leave it (−15) | The Door's price, 500, is carved on the wall. The plate starts creeping. |
| 2 | The Kind Voice | Buy the true way for a vow, price unnamed / go alone and lose bricks in the dark | He names it: a binding vow. He knows it's a bad idea and says so. |
| 3 | The Suit | Climb in (+big, it keeps a piece of him) / go round (−small) | The piece it keeps is his choice of leg or bell-hand, set up for Ep 7. |
| 4 | The Toll | Pay the imp / feed the imp | Feeding it puts the imp on screen beside him from now on. Paying leaves him alone. |
| 5 | The Market | Sell his surname (+150) / keep it and pay to leave (−80) | Biggest swing so far. If he sells, nobody uses his name again, including the Voice. |
| 6 | The Debt Collects | Pay the vow in bricks (−150) / pay it with the bell | A real fork: he reaches Act 3 either poor with the bell, or rich and unable to hear lies. |
| 7 | The Other Walker | Share bricks (−100, gains the red leg and a second ally) / walk past (−10) | The walker tells him the arm takes a brick a day. The counter starts ticking down on its own. |
| 8 | The First Door | The door lets one thing through with him: the imp or the stone arm | No longer two roads to the same place. He loses one of them for good. |
| 9 | The Clock | Sell two years (+90) / take a loan (+200, collected at the Door) | The loan can put him over 500 and still sink him at the threshold. |
| 10 | The Wheel | Let it adapt him (−110, the arm completes) / run (−90, no full arm) | Without the full arm he needs the key and an ally at the Door. |
| 11 | His Own Face | Give the statue his stone arm / fight it (−bricks) | The costs differ. Giving up the arm means someone else must turn the key. |
| 12 | The Door | Pay 500 and walk out / refuse | He can only pay if he has 500. What he has left, and who is with him, is the ending. |

Every count above is a placeholder. If Courtney likes the direction, the ledger gets rebuilt properly around the 500 line, both branches of every vote, and checked the way `ARC.md` section 5 requires.

About 105 words, roughly 38 seconds.

> I woke up made of a thousand bricks. I counted. Twice.
>
> Corridor full of statues. Dead heroes, the lot. And one line carved in the wall. The Door costs five hundred. Hit zero, join the statues.
>
> So. A thousand. Door's five hundred. Easy.
>
> Then this one's arm falls off. Stone. Still warm. Thirty-five bricks, just lying there.
>
> If you've read Hellboy, you know what a stone right hand is for. It opens things that should stay shut.
>
> A. Take the arm. Plus thirty-five. Its eyes just opened, by the way.
>

>
> Comment A or B. Most votes becomes real. Be nice. They're my bricks.
