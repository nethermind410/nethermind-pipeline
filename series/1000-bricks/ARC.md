# 1,000 BRICKS — Series Arc (Season 1)

Once the arc is approved, each episode's script + storyboard is written against it, locked, and only then generated. No disconnected one-offs: every episode inherits from the ones before it and hands something to the ones after.

## 1. THE STORY

A regular dude wakes up in the Nether made of exactly 1,000 bricks, in a corridor lined with statues. The statues were heroes. He has one way out — a Door he has never seen — and one rule he learns in the first room: everything here charges him, and the bricks come off his body.

**What the Nether wants from him.** The Nether does not eat bricks. It eats endings. The corridor is built out of heroes who were chosen apart — every statue is load-bearing, and the Nether needs a steady supply of new stone to keep standing. A hero who is forced apart is weak stone. A hero the crowd *chooses* apart, vote by vote, is strong. That is why nothing in the Nether ever simply attacks him: it offers him choices, and lets the audience do the taking. And when a hero finally stands still forever, the Nether keeps his voice — to guide the next one in.

**The recurring powers.**

- **The Kind Voice (the twin-voice).** A voice in the wall that offers directions, prices and warnings. It is kind. It is patient. It sounds exactly like him. It tells him this is a coincidence. It is not: the voice is the last hero — the previous him, from the previous loop — still speaking from inside the wall he became part of. Every hero hears his own voice because the voice wears the newest hero's voice. It lies about roughly half of what it says, always in the direction of him staying.
- **The Clock.** A room-sized clock that pays in years: bricks now, in exchange for years of his life. Its ticking is heard faintly in the walls from Episode 2 onward, long before he finds the room. The Clock is the Nether's heart — it is counting something down, and in the finale the audience learns it was counting *him*.
- **The statues.** Three truths, revealed in layers: they were heroes (Episode 1). They watch — eyes open when the crowd chooses cruelty (from Episode 1). And they are still in there: the Kind Voice is all of them, speaking as one, in his voice. One statue in the corridor has his face. It is waiting for him to notice.
- **The Door.** The only way out. Talked about, pointed at, lied about, never shown — until the final episode. It does not open for bricks. It opens for a price the Nether chooses at the threshold, and the voice will be there to collect.

**The midpoint turn (Episode 6).** The hidden ledger becomes visible. The debt from Episode 2 is collected, the season's first ally pays back in the same episode, and the voice slips — it calls him by a name that is not his, then corrects itself: *"When I stood where you stand—"* The audience learns the game was never the number. It is what the choices are making of him, and the voice is not a guide. It is a predecessor.

**The endgame (Episodes 9–12).** He sells years to the Clock and comes out richer and greyer. A hunting room binds an adapting wheel to him, and the wheel finishes what Episode 1 started: the looted statue's plate grows into a full stone arm. He finds the statue with his face, and it stands up — it does not want its arm back; it wants him to take its place so it can finally walk. He refuses, and walks to the real Door with the count low, the loadout heavy, and every debt in the season either paid or standing at the threshold with him. The last choice is the crowd's, and it is the only one that was ever going to decide him.

## 2. STRUCTURE — 12 episodes, 3 acts of 4

The count comes from the story, not from habit:

- **The ally/debt rails need runway.** The bible's rule is that an ally pays back 2–4 episodes after the choice that earned it. A season shorter than 12 cannot plant an ally in Act 1, pay it in Act 2, and still have ledger left to collect in Act 3.
- **The loadout needs three movements.** Plate (Act 1) → pieces gained and lost (Act 2) → the stone arm completed and used (Act 3). Compressing that into 8 makes the arm a prop instead of an arc.
- **The voice needs three reveals.** Familiar (Act 1), predecessor (Act 2), his own future (Act 3). One reveal per act; each act ends on one.
- **More than 12 dilutes it.** Past 12, the road tax becomes repetition and the rooms stop escalating. Twelve is where the story runs out — so twelve is the season.

| Act | Episodes | Movement | Act-end turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Act 1 — The Rules** | 1–4 | He learns the economy: bad pays now, good pays later. First loot, first debt, first ally. | He walks out of the Toll with an ally in the ledger and a debt he hasn't heard the price of. |
| **Act 2 — The Ledger Collects** | 5–8 | The prices stop being about bricks and start being about other people. The hidden ledger lands. | The first door he reaches opens onto his own starting corridor. The Nether is a loop. The count is the only thing that has moved. |
| **Act 3 — The Endgame** | 9–12 | Years, adaptation, his own face, the Door. Every planted thread is paid or collected. | The final vote decides whether he walks out or stands still forever. |

## 3. EPISODE MAP

Conventions: **Count at open** is exact and chains — Episode N+1 opens on Episode N's close. The trajectory below is the canonical path (the votes the story is built expecting); where a vote can go the other way, the alternate is named and the rails still hold. Loadout changes are permanent and are added to the hero lock per the bible's bible-first rule. Script lengths are narration word counts (see Production Spec).

### Episode 1 — "The Statue's Arm" · Count at open: 1,000
- **Room / mechanic:** The statue corridor. Looting the dead — the oldest bargain in the genre. (FIXED as written in EP1-STORYBOARD.md.)
- **Dilemma:** A: take the fallen statue's loose arm plate (**+35**) — its eyes open. B: leave it and walk (**−15** road tax) — nothing follows him… yet.
- **Loadout:** A wins → the stone arm plate is on his arm from Episode 2's open. B wins → no plate; the statue's eyes stay closed, and the stone arm thread re-routes (the Market offers him a plate in Episode 5 at a worse price).
- **Threads:** IN — none (season open). OUT — the statues watch (T2); the heart brick established (T10); the plate planted (T9).
- **Script:** ~140 words (≈50s).

### Episode 2 — "The Kind Voice" · Count at open: 1,035 (B path: 985)
- **Room / mechanic:** A voice in the wall selling directions — the guide-who-isn't. Faint ticking under the wall, never named.
- **Dilemma:** A: pay the voice **−30** for the true corridor — and owe it a debt, price unnamed. B: refuse and follow the free echo (**−20** lost in the dark, road tax on top) — no debt, but the voice goes quiet, and quiet is worse.
- **Stakes:** −30 + a debt, or −30 the slow way. The crowd learns both options cost; only one of them owes.
- **Loadout:** No change. Hidden ledger: **DEBT (unnamed)** — and the voice now knows his name, though he never told it.
- **Threads:** IN — T2 (a statue's head has turned, slightly, since Episode 1). OUT — the twin-voice planted (T1: "it sounded like him. He didn't say that part out loud."); the Clock planted as sound only (T8).
- **Script:** ~150 words (≈55s).

### Episode 3 — "The Suit" · Count at open: 995
- **Room / mechanic:** An empty suit of brick armour that wears whoever climbs in — the power-up that drives. It smashes a sealed vault on its way through its one room.
- **Dilemma:** A: climb in — **+50** looted from the vault, but the suit walks where it wants and wears **−25** off him doing it. B: walk around its room the long way (**−25** road and climbing) and leave the vault sealed.
- **Stakes:** Net +25 the dangerous way, or −25 the safe way. First time "bad" is also the *fun* option.
- **Loadout:** A wins → when the suit lets him out, it keeps a piece of him as rent and leaves its own in exchange: **the Suit's gauntlet**, a black plate over one hand that sometimes points at things — at doors, at prices. B wins → no gauntlet; the suit is still standing in its room, and it will be offered again in the Market (Episode 5) at a markup.
- **Threads:** IN — T1 (the voice warned him about this room, and for once it wasn't lying — the bell of truth gets complicated early). OUT — watchers advance (T2: if Episode 1 was looted, the arm-less statue is now two rooms closer than it was).
- **Script:** ~155 words (≈55s).

### Episode 4 — "The Toll" · Count at open: 1,020
- **Room / mechanic:** A bridge, and a small toll imp collecting for a bridge it clearly does not own — the toll that isn't owed. Act 1's heart: the first price that can be answered with kindness.
- **Dilemma:** A: pay the toll **−30** and cross clean. B: refuse the toll and share his food bricks with the imp instead (**−25**) — slower, and the imp eats half of them laughing.
- **Stakes:** B costs almost as much as A. The difference is invisible this episode: B writes **ALLY** in the hidden ledger; A writes nothing.
- **Loadout:** B wins → the imp gives him **the Lie-Bell**: a small brass bell that rings when something near him lies. It rings twice during Episode 4's own closing seconds — once at the imp (fair), once at the wall.
- **Threads:** IN — T8 (the ticking is louder on the bridge; the imp goes quiet when it passes). OUT — the season's first ally planted (T4); the bell planted (T6).
- **Script:** ~160 words (≈60s). **Act 1 ends.**

### Episode 5 — "The Market" · Count at open: 985
- **Room / mechanic:** A market where statues are bought and sold — the price tag room. A dealer prices heroes by the piece.
- **Dilemma:** A: sell the dealer the plate that sits beside his heart brick — **+70**, the biggest single gain of the season. B: refuse and walk out through the market's back rows (**−45** in tolls, breakage and hands grabbing at him on the way).
- **Stakes:** The first truly cruel price the crowd is offered. The narration names it plainly: the plate beside the heart is the one that keeps the heart in.
- **Loadout:** No piece changes hands on the canonical path — but the dealer **clips a price tag to him** on the way out. He cannot get it off. He is marked as stock now. (A wins → the heart-adjacent plate is gone; his chest is visibly opened around the teal brick for the rest of the season, and Episode 12's price gets worse.)
- **Threads:** IN — T2 (the market's stock is all statues; several have price tags in the same hand as his). OUT — the marked thread: things in the dark now know what he's worth (feeds Episodes 8 and 11).
- **Script:** ~165 words (≈60s).

### Episode 6 — "The Debt Collects" · Count at open: 940
- **Room / mechanic:** No new room. The corridor itself — the voice calls in Episode 2's debt, and the season's first ally pays back in the same episode. **ADAPTIVE EPISODE:** built from the actual vote history of Episodes 1–5 (if the crowd ran cruel, the debt version dominates; if kind, the ally version does) — but the rails are fixed: the count lands on 865 either way, and the voice's slip happens either way.
- **Dilemma:** The voice names its price: **the gauntlet** (or, on the B-path, his shadow's weight in bricks, **−80**). A: pay it — hand over the piece and walk lighter. B: refuse — the voice collects anyway, in wear and damage, **−75** net, and keeps the receipt.
- **Stakes:** −75 net on the canonical path: the debt is real money. Inside the same episode, the toll imp holds a collapsing doorway for him and saves him the worse half of the collection — the ally payoff, 2 episodes after it was earned, exactly on the bible's rails.
- **Loadout:** **LOSS — the Suit's gauntlet is gone** (it walks back into the wall on its own fingers). The Lie-Bell is used for the first time on purpose: it rings while the voice names its price, which means part of the price is a lie.
- **Threads:** IN — T3 (the debt, called), T4 (the ally, paid), T6 (the bell, proven). OUT — **T1 turns**: the voice slips — *"When I stood where you stand—"* — and goes silent for the first time all season. **Midpoint.**
- **Script:** ~175 words (≈65s).

### Episode 7 — "The Other Walker" · Count at open: 865
- **Room / mechanic:** No room — the road. Another living hero, worse off than him, limping the other way. The mirror room: everything the crowd has done to him, walking toward him in someone else.
- **Dilemma:** A: share bricks with the walker (**−60**) — enough to drop himself toward the floor. B: refuse and pass (**−10** road tax only) — and the walker keeps walking, and something about his face stays with him.
- **Stakes:** The season's purest good-vs-bad vote. No mechanic, no trick: just a price on kindness, named as one.
- **Loadout:** A wins → on the road after, his own leg plate cracks through; the walker, who saw it happen, comes back and trades him a **bright red mismatched leg plate** off a statue he passed. The repair does not match. It never will. B wins → the crack is patched with corridor rubble; a watcher with the walker's face joins the ones behind him.
- **Threads:** IN — T9 turns: the walker recognises the stone arm plate and is afraid of it. *"The stone takes a brick a day. Didn't it tell you?"* It had not told him. OUT — the arm is not a gift; it is a slow price (feeds Episodes 10–11).
- **Script:** ~165 words (≈60s).

### Episode 8 — "The First Door" · Count at open: 795
- **Room / mechanic:** A door at last — the anywhere-door, the one every story promises. It opens. It is wrong.
- **Dilemma:** A: step through (**−40** — the door charges in wear) and arrive in his own starting corridor, one statue richer or poorer by everything the crowd chose. B: don't step through — turn back into the dark (**−50** searching for another way that isn't there) and arrive at the same corridor from the other end.
- **Stakes:** Both options cost. Both arrive at the same place. The choice is what he loses on the way, and whether the crowd can accept that the Nether cheats.
- **Loadout:** No change. But in the starting corridor, in the row he woke up in, there is a statue he never noticed — because until now he never looked at the faces. **It has his face.** He does not touch it. He does not tell the audience what he saw. The caption does.
- **Threads:** IN — T5's price tag (the marked thread: the door knew his price before he reached it). OUT — **the loop revealed (T7)**; his own statue planted (pays off Episode 11); Act 2 ends.
- **Script:** ~170 words (≈60s). **Act 2 ends.**

### Episode 9 — "The Clock" · Count at open: 745
- **Room / mechanic:** The Clock's room — the clock that pays in years. It offers bricks for years of his life, cash on the table, no tricks in the wording. The trick is what years are.
- **Dilemma:** A: sell two years (**+90**) — the season's biggest clean gain, paid in grey: two of his plates come back grey, at his temples, where the years were. B: refuse and pay the Clock's toll for wasting its time (**−70**) — and hear, on the way out, exactly what the ticking has been counting.
- **Stakes:** A win that feels like a loss against a loss that tells the truth. On the canonical path the crowd sells — and the bell rings as the Clock says *"two years, hardly missed,"* which means the years were worth more than it paid.
- **Loadout:** **The Clock's key** — part of the payment, a small winding key that fits a door he hasn't found. Visual change: **grey temple plates** (the sold years, worn). Both persist to the finale.
- **Threads:** IN — T8 (planted as a sound in Episode 2, paid here). OUT — the key (a Door prerequisite, Episode 12); the years (the finale's last line, either ending).
- **Script:** ~170 words (≈60s).

### Episode 10 — "The Wheel" · Count at open: 835
- **Room / mechanic:** A hunting room — something in it kills by one method, and a Mahoraga-style adapting spirit waits bound in the corner: the wheel that learns. It will adapt him to whatever hurt him last. Every adaptation costs bricks and changes his body.
- **Dilemma:** A: let the wheel turn (**−110** across two adaptations) — the hunter's method stops working on him mid-room. B: refuse the wheel and run the room on his own (**−90** in damage, and the hunter learns *him* instead — it will be faster next time).
- **Stakes:** The crowd votes on whether to let something rewrite his body. The first adaptation is small. The second one isn't: the wheel adapts him to the hunter's crushing grip by turning the looted plate's arm **fully to stone** to the shoulder.
- **Loadout:** **THE STONE ARM COMPLETES** — Episode 1's plate, grown into the full stone arm: the Hellboy arm. It opens some doors and can't hold others. And **the Wheel** itself stays bound to him: a slow ring that turns, once, whenever something is about to hurt him the same way twice. Both are permanent.
- **Threads:** IN — T9 (planted Episode 1, warned Episode 7, completed here). OUT — the arm and the wheel are the finale's tools (Episode 12 prerequisites).
- **Script:** ~180 words (≈65s).

### Episode 11 — "His Own Face" · Count at open: 695
- **Room / mechanic:** The statue with his face stands up. The previous him. It does not want its arm back — the arm was never the point. It wants to walk, and the Nether's arithmetic is simple: one of them stands in the corridor, one of them walks out of this room.
- **Dilemma:** A: give it what it asks — stand still for it, one room's worth (**−55** as it takes bricks off him to remember how walking works). B: fight it (**−55** — the wheel turns once, the stone arm breaks its grip; the cost is identical, the damage is not: fighting cracks the statue's face, and the crack is on *his* face's lines).
- **Stakes:** Same price, two different debts. And the truth, in full, in the narration: the Kind Voice is this statue, and every statue — the voice of every hero who ever stood still, wearing the newest hero's voice. It sounds like him because last time, it *was* him. **From this episode the guardrails are off (§5, zero law):** on a low alternate path, this episode's cost can genuinely take him to zero, and if it does, the statue ending fires here and becomes the finale.
- **Loadout:** No change — deliberately. He faces the Door with exactly what the crowd has made of him: stone arm, wheel, red leg, grey temples, price tag, bell, key, and the teal heart brick, brightest thing on him.
- **Threads:** IN — T1, T2, T7 all pay off here. OUT — everything converges on the Door.
- **Script:** ~185 words (≈70s).

### Episode 12 — "The Door" · Count at open: 640
- **Room / mechanic:** The real Door, shown for the first time. The voice is waiting at the threshold to make its last collection, and the bell rings before it finishes its first sentence.
- **Dilemma — THE FINAL VOTE:** The Door's price is everything but the heart: every brick he has except the teal one and the few that hold it in — he walks out made of almost nothing (**640 → 25**), or he refuses the price and keeps his bricks, and fights the Nether for them. A: **PAY THE DOOR.** B: **REFUSE.**
- **Stakes:** Everything, both ways. See §6, THE END POINT — this episode's ending is locked there.
- **Script:** ~190 words (≈70s) to the choice card; the winning ending is produced as the finale drop (see §6).

## 4. THE LORE MAP — how manga/comics power the Nether

**The principle.** The Nether is where fictional powers end up when their stories die — so lore mechanics are the *physics* of this world, not cameos. Every room runs on one recognisable manga/comic mechanic, re-skinned under a Nether name. The fiction never names the IP on screen; the audience recognising it *is* the engagement. The names and sources live in the episode's caption and pinned comment, where Courtney's lore authority carries the discussion.

**The map — episode → mechanic → source logic → Nether skin:**

- **Ep 1 — The Statue's Arm → Hellboy's Right Hand of Doom.** The stone arm is a key made to open the end of the world. It bookends the series: the stone hand turns the Door's key in Ep 12.
- **Ep 2 — The Kind Voice → JJK binding vows.** The voice can only speak in trades; every promise it makes binds both sides.
- **Ep 3 — The Suit → Venom's symbiote + the arc-reactor problem.** A suit that wears you and feeds — and it wants the teal heart brick, his reactor.
- **Ep 4 — The Toll → Chainsaw Man devil contracts + equivalent exchange.** The imp ally is a contract creature; every favour has a priced clause.
- **Ep 5 — The Market → Death Note / soul-economy logic.** A market where the currency is pieces of people — bricks, years, names.
- **Ep 6 — The Debt Collects → the Ep 2 vow, collected under binding-vow rules.** Break a term, lose the piece the term named — the Suit's gauntlet.
- **Ep 7 — The Other Walker → automail logic (Fullmetal Alchemist).** A limb that isn't his: the mismatched red leg, taken from a walker who didn't make it.
- **Ep 8 — The First Door → the Door-Door fruit (One Piece).** A door that opens anywhere except out — the Nether's tease of the real Door.
- **Ep 9 — The Clock → Soru Soru no Mi (One Piece).** Lifespan as currency: the Clock pays bricks in years and collects the same way; its key is cut from a year of his life.
- **Ep 10 — The Wheel → Mahoraga (JJK).** The adapting wheel: every turn adapts him to what hurt him last, costs bricks, and completes the stone arm.
- **Ep 11 — His Own Face → the loop logic (Attack on Titan's paths / the twin thread).** The Kind Voice is the previous him; his own face on a statue is the reveal.
- **Ep 12 — The Door → the stone hand (Ep 1, Hellboy) is the key.** The Door pays out in everything but the heart brick.

**Standing rule.** One mechanic per episode. The mechanic is named — with its source — in the episode's caption and pinned comment, and never named on screen. New lore enters the series only through this arc first (the bible-first rule): if it isn't in the map, it isn't in an episode until the arc is updated and re-approved.

## 5. THE BRICK LEDGER (SACRED) — AND THE ZERO LAW

**The ledger is sacred.** Every episode's opening count is the previous episode's closing count, exactly. No resets, no quiet gains, no rounding, no "close enough." If a number appears in a script, a counter plate or a storyboard, it chains or the script fails — the ledger is checked like the other gates (see §7). The same law covers everything else that persists: **loadout pieces, debts, injuries and allies carry forward identically** — a piece lost is lost in every later script, a debt unpaid is still owed, an ally earned is still standing in the ledger until the story spends them.

The count at each episode's open. Canonical path; every close is the next open, exactly.

| Episode | Opens at | Net | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — The Statue's Arm | **1,000** | +35 | The crowd takes the plate (A). |
| 2 — The Kind Voice | **1,035** | −40 | Voice's price −30, road −10. A debt opens. |
| 3 — The Suit | **995** | +25 | Vault +50, the suit's wear −25. The season's easy high. |
| 4 — The Toll | **1,020** | −35 | Shared food bricks −25, road −10. An ally opens, invisibly. |
| 5 — The Market | **985** | −45 | Refuses the cruel price; the market takes its cut anyway. |
| 6 — The Debt Collects | **940** | −75 | The debt is collected; the ally's save is already inside this number. |
| 7 — The Other Walker | **865** | −70 | Kindness −60, road −10. A red leg plate comes back. |
| 8 — The First Door | **795** | −50 | The wrong door charges −40, road −10. The loop. |
| 9 — The Clock | **745** | +90 | Two years sold. The win that greys him. |
| 10 — The Wheel | **835** | −140 | Two adaptations −110, the hunter's damage −30. |
| 11 — His Own Face | **695** | −55 | The statue takes its tuition, either way. |
| 12 — The Door | **640** | → **25** or → **0** | The final vote. |

The shape: two early wins keep the crowd brave (Episodes 1 and 3), the middle grinds down through the 900s and 800s as the ledger collects, the Clock's +90 at Episode 9 is the last false hope — it puts him at his Act 3 peak precisely so the Wheel can take the biggest single fall of the season (−140) and leave him visibly coming apart: cracked torso (from ~940), a gauntlet-shaped absence (Episode 6), a leg that doesn't match (Episode 7), grey at the temples (Episode 9), one arm of stone (Episode 10). By Episode 12 the number says 640 and his body says less. He reaches the Door held together by repairs, with the teal heart brick the brightest thing on him.

**Closing counts, canonical path (each is the next episode's open):** Ep 1 closes **1,035** · Ep 2 closes **995** · Ep 3 closes **1,020** · Ep 4 closes **985** · Ep 5 closes **940** · Ep 6 closes **865** · Ep 7 closes **795** · Ep 8 closes **745** · Ep 9 closes **835** · Ep 10 closes **695** · Ep 11 closes **640** · Ep 12 closes **25** (Door) or **0** (statue).

Alternate-path note: if Episode 1 votes B, Episode 2 opens at **985** and every later open shifts by the running difference; the act rails (Act 1 ends ~985±50, Act 2 ends ~745±50, Episode 12 opens between 590 and 690) are what the per-episode ledgers must land on, whichever way individual votes fall. The story beats do not move; only the numbers flex inside their rails.

### The zero law

**Zero = he becomes a statue = the story ends.** Not a setback, not a cliffhanger — the end. The whole season is built so the audience feels that on every vote while the construction keeps the story alive until it's time.

- **(a) Tuned costs (Episodes 1–10).** No single A-or-B outcome, on either branch, can take him to zero before the endgame. Worst-case branches are costed against the lowest possible incoming count on the rails, with margin: through Episode 10 he can be hurt toward ~600 on a bad run, never under it. The risk must FEEL total — the narration never says he's safe — while the arithmetic guarantees the story survives to its endgame.
- **(b) Pressure valves are story choices, never resets.** If a bad run of votes pushes the count low, the story — never the spreadsheet — offers him air, and every valve is itself a voted dilemma with a price:
  - **Sell back a loadout piece.** The Market (Episode 5) and the road after it will buy pieces back: bricks now, the piece gone forever. The ultimate version waits in the endgame: sell the stone arm itself and walk to the Door with two ordinary hands.
  - **A Nether loan.** Bricks now, a debt the Clock collects — staged in Episode 9, where the Clock offers a loan alongside the years deal, at a rate the bell has opinions about. A loan taken is a debt in the ledger, collected at the worst moment, like every debt in this series.
  - **An ally's gift.** An ally in the ledger can gift him bricks — at a price the ally names (a favour owed, a door held shut instead of open, a name given up). Staged as a live option from Episode 7 onward wherever an earned ally stands.
  No valve fires automatically. If the crowd never votes for one, he stays poor. The valves exist so a cornered season always has a *story* way out — never a mechanical one.
- **(c) From Episode 11, the guardrails come OFF.** Zero is genuinely possible. If the live ledger reaches zero in Episode 11 or Episode 12 — through the statue's tuition on a low alternate path, or the refusal fight in the finale — the statue ending fires immediately, mid-episode, and becomes the ending (see §6). From the penultimate episode the construction no longer protects him. Only the votes do.

## 6. THE END POINT (LOCKED)

This section is definite. The season ends one of two ways, the final vote on Episode 12 chooses which, and both endings are written here in full so the per-episode scripts can aim at them from Episode 1.

### What the final episode must contain
1. Episode 11's consequence and vote split, on screen, per the standing episode shape.
2. The counter plate reading **640** at the open (canonical path).
3. The full loadout visible in one frame: stone arm, wheel, red leg plate, grey temples, price tag, the key in his stone hand, the bell silent — and the teal heart brick, lit.
4. The Door, shown for the first time in the season: no build-up shot of it has been spent before this episode.
5. The voice at the threshold, attempting one last collection — and the bell's last ring exposing its final lie: that the Door needs his heart brick. It doesn't. It never did. The heart is the one thing the price excludes, and the voice wanted him to offer it anyway.
6. The final choice spoken in full by the narration before the card appears: **A: PAY THE DOOR — everything but the heart. Walk out made of almost nothing. B: REFUSE — keep every brick. Fight the Nether for them.**
7. The card holds with a longer vote window than any other episode (72 hours), stated in the caption: this one decides him.
8. The winning ending is produced and released as the finale drop — **Episode 12, Part 2: "What You Chose"** — within the normal episode cadence. Only the voted ending is produced as canon. (The unchosen ending stays in this document. It is never released as content; it is the road the crowd didn't take, and its existence is what makes the vote real.)

### Ending A — he pays, and gets out (final count: 25)
He puts the Clock's key in the stone hand — the only hand that can turn it — and pays. The bricks come off him in the Door's light: the red leg, the grey temples, plate after plate, the wheel last, turning once as it goes, adapting him to leaving. He steps through made of 25 bricks and the heart. Outside is not a kingdom. It is a normal room at giant scale, morning light, a wooden floor — the real world, where he is very small and completely free. The narration's last line is the Clock's receipt, finally understood: *"Two years, hardly missed. He had a lot of life left to be small in."* Final frame: the teal brick, still lit, in an open doorway of ordinary light.
**What must be true for this ending to land:** he holds the key (Episode 9) or the completed stone arm (Episode 10) — on the canonical path he holds both; the imp ally is alive in the ledger (Episode 4's B, paid forward in Episode 6) to hold the Door from the other side while he pays; the Episode 6 debt is settled, or the voice's threshold collection fails because the bell exposes it (Episode 12, beat 5). If a season's actual votes removed one of these, the ending uses the ones that remain — the script for Episode 12 names which, explicitly, so the audience hears its own history open the Door.

### Ending B — he refuses, and stands still (final count: 0)
He keeps his bricks and fights. He is good at it now — stone arm, wheel turning, every adaptation the crowd ever bought him firing at once — and it does not matter, because the Nether is not an enemy, it is a place, and a place cannot lose a fight. The bricks come off him the other way: not paid, taken. The count runs down on screen, past every number the crowd ever cheered, and the last brick to go grey is the teal one. He stands in the corridor, in the row where the statue with his face used to stand — that space is empty now, and the audience understands why before the caption says it: *"The corridor is one statue richer. It is always one statue richer."* **The statue ending's last beat is his statue**: still, grey, the teal brick dark in its chest, in the row he woke up in — and somewhere in the walls, the Clock stops ticking. And starts again. That is where the season ends.
**What must be true for this ending to land:** only that the crowd chooses B — or ties (ties go to the worse option; the Nether breaks ties, and refusal is the worse option) — **or that the live count reaches zero in Episode 11 or 12 under the zero law**, in which case this ending fires early and becomes the finale. No prerequisite can save him from a B vote: that is the point of it, and the Episode 12 narration says so before the card — if they refuse the price, the price is him.
**Continuation seed (designed, not decided):** a final beat exists and is fully designed, held for Courtney's call at finale lock — after the last beat above, a new figure wakes on the corridor floor, and a kind voice in the wall, *his* voice now, says the kindest thing it knows how to say. Including it seeds a second season; excluding it changes nothing in this season's canon — the statue ending is complete without it. The mechanism is designed here so either choice is producible at lock time. Courtney decides later; this arc does not force the call.

## 7. ALIGNMENT RULES — for every per-episode script written from this arc

Every script, no exceptions, must carry:

1. **Last vote's consequence + the split.** The episode opens on the result of the previous vote, with the percentage on screen and in the narration's first lines. (Episode 1 excepted: it opens on the waking.)
2. **The current brick count.** The count in the script, the counter plate, and the narration agree exactly, and the open equals the previous episode's close. Any number in a script that doesn't chain is a failed script.
3. **The current loadout.** Every piece gained, grafted, bound or lost to date appears in the storyboard's hero description (added to the lock, bible-first) and is visible or accounted for. Nothing appears from nowhere; nothing vanishes quietly.
4. **One thread advanced.** At least one standing thread (voice, statues, debt, ally, bell, clock, loop, arm, heart) moves forward this episode, per the episode map.
5. **One thread planted.** At least one new element is planted that a later episode in the map is scheduled to pay off. No plant without a payoff already in the arc; no payoff without a plant.
6. **Narration first (format lock).** Episodes are voiced, 45–75 seconds, narrated in Courtney's voice (ElevenLabs clone). Scripts are written to be SPOKEN — the story is carried by the narration; captions support only. Word budget: ~125 words ≈ 45s, ~165 words ≈ 60s, ceiling ~190 words ≈ 75s at his voice's pace. Structure: **hook line** (the first spoken line pays off the last vote or opens the loop) → **story beats** (the room, the mechanic, the price, each landing before the next) → **the dilemma** (both options spoken in full, with their prices) → **choice card** (A-or-B on screen with the count as it stands). The A-or-B lands only after the narration has made the stakes understood — nobody votes on a story they don't know.
7. **The standing sequence.** Arc approved → per-episode script + storyboard written and locked (Courtney's agreement) → only then generation. No generation before scripts are locked. No exceptions, no "just one still."
8. **The bible's standing gates still apply:** hero description verbatim in every prompt; the binary per-feature consistency gate against the master; banned-word sweep at zero before anything is shown; the finished episode watched end to end; nothing posts without Courtney's tap.
9. **The ledger check (sacred).** Before a script locks, its numbers are checked against the brick ledger: opening count = previous closing count, every movement priced inside the bible's ranges, closing count recorded. Loadout, debts, injuries and allies are checked the same way — present if the ledger says present, absent if it says gone. A script that fails the ledger check does not lock.
10. **The zero law is inherited.** Scripts for Episodes 1–10 must keep both branches survivable per §5(a) — no outcome zeroes him early, however the narration sells the danger. Pressure valves (sell-back, Nether loan, ally's gift) may only ever appear as voted dilemmas with named prices, never as narration gifts or quiet fixes. Scripts for Episodes 11–12 are written guardrails-off: if the live count can reach zero, the script must contain the statue-ending firing point, and the finale script is checked against both locked endings in §6 before it locks.

## 8. EPISODE PRODUCTION SPEC

- **Format:** voiced brick stop-motion, vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, 12 fps from posed stills. No image-to-video AI.
- **Length:** 45–75 seconds per episode (finale drop may run to the top of that range). Narration is the spine; the picture is posed to the narration, not the other way round.
- **Voice:** ElevenLabs clone "Courtney." Scripts are written in his cadence to be spoken by it — story carried by the narration, captions as support and for the on-screen furniture (counter, percentages, the card).
- **Narration-first structure, every episode:**
  1. **Hook line** — one spoken line that pays off the last vote (Episodes 2+) or opens the loop (Episode 1: he wakes).
  2. **Story beats** — the room and its lore mechanic introduced through what it *does*, the price stated, the hidden-ledger movement (ally/debt/watcher) shown in the picture even when the narration doesn't name it yet.
  3. **The dilemma** — both options spoken in full, prices attached, the creature/object waiting. The count flickers.
  4. **Choice card** — A-or-B full-frame, count as it stands, vote invitation ("Comment A or B — most voted becomes real"), loop back to the opening geography where the format allows.
- **Standing sequence (no exceptions):** this arc approved by Courtney → per-episode script written to the arc and the word budget → storyboard written to the locked script → Courtney's agreement on script + storyboard → generation (stills, gate, animation) → Courtney's publish tap.
- **Ledgers:** each episode folder keeps its brick ledger (open, movements, close) and loadout ledger; the next episode's script is checked against both before it is locked.
