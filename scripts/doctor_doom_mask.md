## Script

**[COLD OPEN]**

Doctor Doom doesn't wear the mask to hide a scar.

He wears it because Reed Richards was right. Once. About some maths.

That's it. That's the villain. The greatest in comics, sixty years of trying to take over the world, and underneath all of it is one sentence he cannot make himself say.

I was wrong.

And the proof is this. The one time he finally says it, his face heals.

Let me show you.

**[THE BOY]**

Start in Latveria. A Romani camp. A boy called Victor.

His mother, Cynthia, is a sorceress. She makes a deal with Mephisto for the power to protect her people, and it costs her her life and her soul.

His father, Werner, is a healer. A baron's wife is dying. Werner can't save her, so he runs, with Victor, into the mountains in winter. He wraps his son in his own coat. Victor lives. Werner doesn't.

So before this boy is a teenager he has learned two things. The people in charge will kill you for failing. And the people you love get taken.

He decides nobody will ever have that power over him again.

**[THE MACHINE]**

He's a genius. Science and sorcery, both, self-taught, out of his mother's old belongings. Good enough that an American university comes to find him.

And that's where he meets Reed Richards.

Victor is building a machine. Not a weapon. A machine to reach the place where his mother is. He wants to speak to her. That's the whole project. A son trying to call home.

Reed looks at his notes and tells him the calculations are off.

Now. Victor could check. It would take an afternoon. He's the second cleverest man in the building and the cleverest is offering to help.

He doesn't check.

He turns it on. It blows up in his face.

**[THE FACE]**

And you know this bit, because I've told you before. The face.

In the original drawings it's a small scar. In 1985, John Byrne makes that the point of the story. The bandages come off and it's one mark. Eye to chin. On anyone else, nothing.

On Victor, it's the end of the world. Because it's proof. Every time he looks in a mirror, the mirror says Reed was right.

So he walks to the mountains. He finds an order of monks and has them forge him a suit of armour. And he puts the mask on before the metal has cooled.

That's what ruins his face. Not the accident. The mask.

He would rather burn his own face off than look at one small mistake.

**[THE SENTENCE]**

From that day, everything Doom does is the same sentence said in different ways.

I was not wrong.

It was Reed's fault. Reed tampered with the machine. Reed was jealous. And if the whole world would simply do as Doom says, the whole world would see he was right all along.

That's why he doesn't want to destroy the world. He wants to run it. Destroying it proves nothing. Running it perfectly proves everything.

**[THE CASE FOR HIM]**

And here's why he's the best villain Marvel has. He's almost right.

Look at Latveria. His people are fed. They're safe. No crime, no hunger, no war that reaches them. They're also not free, and he would not understand why you'd bring that up.

Look at his mother. In Triumph and Torment, we find out that once a year, every year, Doom fights the Devil for her soul. And loses. Every year. And goes back.

When he finally wins, with Doctor Strange beside him, he does it with a trick. He pretends to sell Strange out. His mother sees it, refuses to be saved that way, and that refusal is what frees her. She goes on to somewhere better.

Hating him.

He gets the one thing he ever wanted and loses her in the same breath. And he calls that a victory, because the alternative is admitting there was a better way to do it.

Then there's Valeria. Sue Storm is in labour and it's going wrong, and Reed, the cleverest man alive, can't fix it. So they call Doom. And Doom saves the baby. His price is that he names her. Valeria. After the only woman he ever loved.

**[THE CASE AGAINST]**

Don't get comfortable.

Because in the story called Unthinkable, he goes and finds that woman. The real Valeria. And he trades her to demons for power. He wears what's left of her as armour.

Then he sends Reed's son to Hell.

So no. He is not a misunderstood hero. He is a man who will pay any price at all, as long as he's not the one who was wrong. Other people are the price.

**[THE PROOF]**

And then, 2015. Secret Wars.

Everything dies. Every universe. And when it's over, one man has saved what's left and stitched it into a single world.

Doom.

He's God. Actually God. And it works. Badly, cruelly, held together with fear, but it works, and nobody else managed it.

Then Reed turns up. And at the very end, the two of them, face to face, Reed asks him the question that's been sitting there since that university lab.

Do you think I'd have done it better?

And Doom says yes.

The sentence. After fifty years of comics, he says it out loud.

And when the world is put back together, Victor von Doom takes off the mask.

His face is healed.

That's the whole character on one page. The scar was never the injury. The injury was that he couldn't say it.

**[WHY NOW]**

Which brings us to the 18th of December.

Robert Downey Jr. is Doom. And the trailer already tells you which Doom he's playing. Sue Storm is narrating his past. He's telling the heroes they've been living stolen lives.

That's not a man who wants to burn it all down. That's a man who thinks he's the only one who can fix it, and has the maths to prove it.

So when you watch it, watch for one thing. Whether this Doom is capable of the sentence.

If he is, he can be saved. If he isn't, nobody in that film is safe.

**[THE WAR]**

Your turn.

If Doom could run the world, and it worked, fed, safe, not free, do you take the deal?

Scrap it out in the comments. I know where I stand.

The sixty seconds on what really happened to his face is on the channel. Go watch it. Subscribe if you're staying for the next one.

Done.
