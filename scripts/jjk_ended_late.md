## Script

**[COLD OPEN]**

Jujutsu Kaisen was supposed to end in 2023.

It ended in September 2024. Nineteen months late.

And the person who kept getting the date wrong was the only person alive who knew how it ended.

So what happened? He didn't run out of story. He didn't lose the plot. He knew the last chapter before most of you had heard the name Gojo. What he didn't have was the road to it. And the road is where this whole thing went sideways.

**[THE RECEIPTS]**

Start with the receipts.

February 2021. Gege Akutami goes on Japanese TV. The manga is about a hundred and forty chapters deep. Shibuya has only just finished wrecking everybody. And he says it'll probably end within two years.

Probably. Keep an eye on that word. It does a lot of work in this story.

December 2022. Jump Festa. His message to the fans: stay with me for one more year. Probably.

December 2023. A year later. Still going. Gojo is already dead and the manga is still going. And this time the message says this is probably, definitely the last Jump Festa while the series is running.

Third call. First one he got right. Chapter 271. The 30th of September 2024.

Two misses and a hit. From the author. About his own book.

**[THE TURN]**

Now here's what makes that interesting instead of just funny.

In the afterword to Jujutsu Kaisen 0, he says the final chapter was already decided. Already. Before the main series had found its feet. He just didn't know how the dots would connect.

That's a man with a destination and no map.

And three things stretched that map. Not one of them is "he got lazy".

**[ONE — SHIBUYA BROKE THE BOARD]**

One. Shibuya broke the board.

Before Shibuya, this series has a ceiling. One man holding it up. Gojo exists, so nothing properly terrible is allowed to happen for long.

Then Shibuya puts him in a box.

And everything that ceiling was holding up comes down at once. Nanami, gone. Nobara, down. Sukuna let off the lead and a piece of the city just missing. Kenjaku with his mask off. Yuji walking out of it with an execution order and a body count that isn't his but feels like it is.

That's fifty-odd chapters that end with the board flipped over.

So when he says "two years" in February 2021, look where he's standing. Shibuya has just finished. He's in the rubble, guessing how long the rebuild takes. Nobody guesses that right.

**[TWO — THE CULLING GAME]**

Two. The Culling Game.

To get Gojo out of the box, the story needs a key. To get the key, it needs a game. And the game needs rules, and colonies, and points, and players.

So in they come. Hakari. Kashimo. Higuruma. Takaba. A whole new cast, most of them strong enough to carry an arc.

And here's the problem with this series. You can't rush a Jujutsu Kaisen fight. Every fight is an argument about rules. What does the technique do. What does it cost. What's the loophole. Skip that and it's just two people glowing at each other.

So every new player needs a technique, a limit, and a fight that proves both. That's not padding. That's the price of the power system he built.

Now put the second receipt next to it. December 2022, "one more year, probably". At that point Gojo is still in the box. Sukuna hasn't even taken Megumi yet. The two biggest moves in the entire endgame haven't happened.

One more year was never on.

**[THREE — THE FIGHT HE COULDN'T RUSH]**

Three. The fight he couldn't rush.

Gojo comes out of the box, and the manga gives you the fight it's been promising since chapter one. The strongest of today against the strongest in history. Fourteen chapters. Domain against domain until both of them are running on fumes.

And Gojo loses.

Then it doesn't stop. Because Sukuna is still standing, and now everyone else has to take a turn. Kashimo. Higuruma. Yuta. Maki. Then Yuta again, in a body that isn't his. Then Yuji, at the very end, with a domain of his own.

From the first page of Gojo against Sukuna to the page Sukuna finally falls is forty-six chapters. About sixteen months.

Read that back against December 2022. He said the whole rest of the manga would take a year. The last fight alone took longer than that.

That's not a writer stalling. That's a writer who reached the fight the series was built for and refused to cut it short.

**[THE VERDICT]**

So here's the verdict.

Jujutsu Kaisen didn't end late because he didn't know the ending. It ended late because he did. He knew exactly where it had to land, and every time he got closer, the road needed one more piece before the landing would hold.

And here's the part that stings. After all that overtime, what do people say about the ending?

That it felt rushed.

Forty-six chapters on the last fight. Three chapters to say goodbye. He spent the extra time on the war and almost none of it on the peace. Even the book knew it. The final volume came out that Christmas with an epilogue added, because three chapters wasn't enough.

Nineteen months late, and still called rushed. Both of those are true at once. That's Jujutsu Kaisen.

**[WHY NOW]**

And this matters right now, because the anime is walking the same road.

Jump Festa is back on the 19th and 20th of December, and the Jujutsu Kaisen stage is promising the latest on Season 4. The anime is heading into the exact stretch that blew the manga's schedule apart.

So when someone tells you how many seasons are left, remember who else tried to call it.

**[THE WAR]**

Right. Your turn.

Would you take the ending on time, in 2023, and lose the fight in Shinjuku? Or late, the way we got it, with all forty-six chapters of it?

Pick one. Scrap it out in the comments. I know mine.
