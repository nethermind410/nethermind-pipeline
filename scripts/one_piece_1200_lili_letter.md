## Script

**[COLD OPEN]**

A king was killed over this letter.

Not over a weapon. Not over a throne. Over a signature at the bottom of a page.

And at the end of chapter 1199, Robin picks up a letter from the same woman and says five words.

"I'm going to read the letter."

Chapter 1200 is on Sunday. So before it lands, here's everything that letter has to answer. Three questions. And then I'll tell you what I think it says.

**[WHO IS LILI]**

First. Who wrote it.

Eight hundred years ago, twenty kings win a war and build the World Government. Nineteen of those families move up to Mary Geoise and become the Celestial Dragons.

One doesn't. The Nefertari family. Alabasta. They go home.

Except their queen doesn't.

Queen Lili is one of the twenty. She's there at the start of the world as we know it. And then she vanishes. Alabasta's own records say she never came back. The World Government says it has no record of her staying.

Eight hundred years of nobody knowing where she went.

**[THE REVERIE]**

Now go back to the Reverie.

King Cobra walks in to see the Five Elders with one thing on his mind. He wants to know what happened to her. And he wants to know what one letter means. Because his family has been keeping a letter from Lili for eight centuries.

And then the room changes. Because someone who is not supposed to exist sits down on the Empty Throne.

Imu.

And Imu says her name. Lili. Like it's personal.

Then Imu tells him something no one alive was meant to hear. The Poneglyphs, the stones Robin has been chasing since she was eight, are scattered across the world because of Lili. Imu calls it her great mistake.

And then Imu asks the question. Was it a mistake? Or was it planned?

So Cobra answers with the only thing he has. The signature on the letter.

Nefertari D. Lili.

D.

Watch Imu's face in that panel. Surprise first. Then rage.

And Cobra doesn't leave that room alive.

**[WHY ROBIN]**

Here's why it had to be Robin who finds it.

Go all the way back to Alabasta. The royal tomb. Robin and Cobra standing in front of the Poneglyph his family had guarded for eight hundred years. She reads it. Crocodile demands to know what it says. And she lies to his face.

That's where these two stories first touch. Robin, the last person alive who can read the stones. Cobra, the king of the family that hid one.

He spent the rest of his life wanting to know why. He died asking.

And the answer is now in Robin's hands, in Elbaf, on the far side of the world from where he asked it.

**[THREE QUESTIONS]**

So. Three questions that letter has to answer.

One. What is the D.

We've been asking since Loguetown. Luffy has it. Roger had it. Blackbeard has it. And now a founding queen of the World Government has it. One of the twenty. On the winning side.

That shouldn't be possible. Imu called the D. the name of the people who once stood against them. So what is a D. doing among the people who won?

Two. Mistake, or plan.

Imu doesn't know. Think about that. The oldest thing in this world has had eight hundred years to work it out and still had to ask a king from the desert.

If it was a mistake, Lili is a footnote. If it was a plan, she's the reason this entire story can happen. No scattered stones, no Road Poneglyphs, no Laugh Tale, no Roger, no Luffy.

Three. Where did she go.

She never went home. She never stayed. And Imu still says her name like that.

Whatever is between those two, it isn't politics.

**[MY CALL]**

Right. My call.

It was a plan. And the letter isn't a confession. It's instructions.

Two reasons.

First. You don't scatter indestructible stones across four seas by accident. That's not a slip. That's a delivery route.

Second. Look at what her family did next. They took one of those stones, buried it under their own palace, and guarded it for eight hundred years without knowing what it said. Generation after generation. Nobody does that on a hunch. They do it because someone they trusted told them to.

I think that someone was Lili. I think the letter is the telling.

And if I'm right, chapter 1200 doesn't just explain a missing queen. It tells us the first person who ever betrayed the World Government was sitting at the table when it was founded.

**[WHAT TO WATCH FOR]**

Three things to watch on Sunday.

Who the letter is addressed to. A letter to family is one story. A letter to whoever finds it is another.

Whether the D. gets a meaning, or just more weight.

And whether we get a flashback. Because if this chapter opens eight hundred years ago, we are in the Void Century, and nothing after it is the same.

**[THE WAR]**

So pick your side before Sunday proves one of us wrong.

Mistake, or plan?
