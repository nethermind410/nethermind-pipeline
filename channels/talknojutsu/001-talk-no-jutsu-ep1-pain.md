## 6. The argument in plain words (for the script writer)

**What Naruto actually said** (chapters 444, 447, 448; episodes 169, 174):
- Before any speech he asks to hear Nagato's story. He says he can't forgive Nagato but wants to talk before he decides.
- After the story: he understands the reasoning and even sees some truth in it, and he rejects it.
- He will not kill Nagato. Not killing him is the first step.
- He quotes the hero of Jiraiya's first book: he will break the curse, and if there is such a thing as peace he will find it.
- He admits he does not know how yet (stated on the episode 174 page).

**What else changed Nagato's mind:**
- Jiraiya's book. The hero's line is Nagato's own, said to Jiraiya when Jiraiya had writer's block. The book is dedicated to Nagato. The hero is named Naruto, and Naruto was named after him.
- Minato. He never speaks to Nagato, but he is why Naruto walks in calm. He stops Naruto freeing the Nine-Tails, tells him Pain is a product of his era, and tells him to find his own answer.
- Shared pain and a shared teacher. Nagato says Naruto reminds him of himself when he was younger.
- The deed. Naruto had already turned down help to kill Nagato before he said a word to him.

**Strongest case it "should not have worked":**
1. Naruto has no plan. Silent in 436, still no method in 448.
2. Nagato held this belief for most of his life, killed Jiraiya for it and levelled a village an hour earlier. One conversation is thin.
3. The turn leans on a name in a book. Nagato himself says it feels like someone set it all up.
4. There is no bill. The Rinne Rebirth undoes the deaths, and Naruto alone decides what happens to Nagato.
5. It only happens after every one of Pain's bodies is destroyed. The talk follows the win.

**The verdict the video commits to:**
- **Did it work? Yes.** And it should have, for a reason people miss: this was not persuasion. Nagato was not argued out of his belief. He was shown the belief he had before Yahiko died, in his own words, carried by a kid with the same teacher and real pain of his own, who had just declined to kill him.
- **Was Naruto right? Half.** Right about Nagato and right to refuse the kill. But what he offered was a promise, not an answer, and Nagato paid for that promise with his life. The story does not hide this: Nagato's last words are a warning about what Naruto still has to face.
- Series scorecard for Episode 1: **WORKED: YES. RIGHT: HALF.**
