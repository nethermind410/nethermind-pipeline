# How Courtney's Shorts talk (25 Nov 2026)

He asked for this after rewriting the Dragon Ball Z Short himself: "analyse the comic and words used in this for when
we do more. Like it's funny and rude and chaotic. Swearing and brash."

Source: his own script in `2026-11-25-dragon-ball-z-shopping-list.md`, set against the four drafts Claude wrote
before it and what he said about them. One script is a small sample. Add to this file every time he rewrites one.

## The feel

Pub-table energy: someone who knows the lore cold, telling it badly on purpose. Rude,
quick, a bit unhinged, never explaining himself. The facts are real. The delivery takes the piss out of them.

## What he does, with his own lines

| Move | His line | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Pull them in, don't dare them** | "Come walk the aisles." | An invitation into a scene. He cut "Don't believe me?" |
| **Build them up, then "just"** | "Prince of all Saiyans, proudest man in anime, and his name is just the word vegetable." | Two big titles in a row, then one small word knocks it over. |
| **The wink** | "Which used to mean something entirely different." | A rude thought pointed at and left alone. He never says what it is. The viewer does the work. |
| **Flip the stock phrase** | "which is, you didn't guess it, vegetables" | Takes a phrase everyone knows and turns it inside out. |
| **The two or three word button** | "Food for thought." / "Carrots are tough." | A short tag after a longer line. Often an everyday saying that happens to be about the subject. |
| **Then and now, about himself** | "Carrots used to make me think I could see in the dark. Now all I see is Kakarot yelling at the freezer." | A daft memory from real life, then the lore crashing into it. First person. |
| **The fond insult** | "Little fucker couldn't even put rabbit food on ice." | Swears at the villain the way you would at a friend. Gives the hero a worse nickname than the lore does. |
| **Take the saying literally** | "put ... on ice" / "dressed for the occasion" | A phrase that means one thing, used where it also means the silly thing. |
| **The riddle** | "What happens when you throw a vegetable in a cupboard with Bulma? Trunks and Bra, that's what." | Asks it like a bad joke, answers it flat. |
| **Add it up** | "That's a whole outfit and lunch." | Totals the nonsense as if it were a sensible list. |
| **The fake moral** | "So never turn your nose up at a salad, and always be dressed for the occasion." | Ends like a lesson. The lesson is useless. |

## The rhythm

- A long, loose line, then a short one. Run-on, then punch.
- It jumps by what the last thing reminds him of, not by logic: carrots, seeing in the dark, Kakarot, the freezer.
  That is the chaos, and it is on purpose.
- Fragments are fine. So is starting with "And" or "So".
- No joining words that sound like an essay. He cut "which makes", "because" and "that's how you end up with".

## What he cut from Claude's drafts

| Claude wrote | Why it went |
|---|---|
| "Japanese for vegetable, scrambled" | Explaining. The name swap on screen does that job; the voice stays funny. |
| "Don't believe me?" | A challenge to the viewer. He invites instead. |
| "So always eat your veggies" | A tidy moral. His ending is a moral that helps nobody. |
| Bolted-on puns ("tossed across the universe", "rooting for Earth") | A pun has to come out of the thought, not be stuck on the end of a fact. |

## Rules for writing the next one

1. **Start from a real, checked fact, then be rude about it.** The facts table still has to hold.
2. **Never explain the joke or the pun.** If the fact needs stating, the screen states it.
3. **At least one thing from his own life** per Short: a memory, a habit, what he thought as a kid.
4. **One wink per Short at most**, pointed at and left alone.
5. **Swear where a person would**, at the villain or the absurdity. One or two, landed, beats a string of them.
6. **End on a useless moral and a button of two to four words.**
7. **Read it aloud.** If any line sounds like a caption, a list item or a narrator, rewrite it.
8. **Still locked:** his four banned words, no em dashes, Australian spelling, 2nd Gear naming. His own script is
   used character-exact.

## Where swearing can hurt reach

Keep it out of the title, the thumbnail and the opening seconds, and out of the on-screen text. YouTube has limited
ads and reach in the past for strong language right at the start or in titles. That is from memory, not a page that
was opened today; check the current rule before leaning on it.

## The workflow this suggests

He writes better punchlines than Claude does. So: Claude brings the checked facts, the hook, the order and a rough
draft in this voice; he roughs it up; Claude smooths the joins without touching the jokes.
